# Conventional Signs for Japanese Maps — MID translation (1920s)

A 16-page typescript legend of Japanese topographic map signs and military
conventional signs, prepared by the U.S. Army Military Intelligence Division
(file MID 2023-707) from Japanese sources. U.S. government work — public domain.

| **Direct page link** | `https://catalog.archives.gov/id/248350588?objectPage=77` (through 92) |
| **Extracted** | 2026-08-01, machine vision transcription of the typescript; symbols are described in brackets, labels are verbatim period phrasing |

## Why this is here

This legend is the notation system running through the project's period sources.
It decodes the unit shorthand in the JACAR combat accounts (`3i` = 3rd Infantry
Regiment, `II/1i` = 2nd Battalion 1st Infantry Regiment, headquarters and
detachment symbols, billeting/bivouac conventions), and its verbatim 1920s
English labels are a **period-phrasing reference** for our translations — when
choosing between modern and contemporary renderings of a Japanese military term,
prefer the phrasing attested here (e.g. "trench mortar", "machinegun",
"alarm quarters").

Symbol drawings are described textually in brackets — consult the page images at
the NAID link above for the actual glyphs; per the project's data policy the
scans themselves are not committed. Attribution: National Archives and Records
Administration; no known copyright restrictions (U.S. government work).

**Conventional Signs.  Japanese Army.** (Published Aug. 23, 1924)

**General Instruction.**

1. Conventional signs of both armies are usually colored.
The enemy is usually shown in red and our forces in indigo.
2. Abbreviations other than these signs are used in this book where the meaning is clear and known. Explanations or special signs may supplement them.
3. The letter G is attached to the symbol for units of the Guards Division.
For an independent unit, the letter S is added on the end of the name of the unit.
Reserves are shown with one line ( ___ ) drawn below the abbreviated sign.
National Army troops are shown with two ( ═══ ) drawn below the abbreviated sign.
4. To show boundaries of districts or limits of fortified places a line is used. Directions of shooting, points of attacks or changes of direction of troops are shown by an arrow thus: ——→ or ---→
5. Roman numerals are used for the battalions of a regiment only. Arabic numerals for all the other units, thus: 2i - the 2nd infantry, 18P - the 18th engineers.
6. The number in brackets following the sign of a detachment, machine gun, cannon, aeroplane, etc., shows the number thereof. Thus

| Sign | Meaning |
|---|---|
| i(五大) | 5 battalions of Infantry |
| K(四中) | 4 squadrons of Cavalry |
| A(三大) | 3 battalions of Artillery |
| P(一大) [the 大 has a pencil circle around it] | 1 platoon of Engineers |
| [arrow with solid ball, MG sign](2) | Machine guns |
| [three-pronged cannon sign, 山-like](8) | Cannons |
| [bowtie/propeller sign ▷◁](2) | Aeroplanes |

7. The slanting line indicates that the units to the left are part of the units to the right, thus:

III/2i　3rd battalion of the 2nd infantry regiment.

1/16 2/5K　1 squad. 2nd. squadron, 5th cav. regt. [the "1" is typed above "16" as a stacked fraction]
