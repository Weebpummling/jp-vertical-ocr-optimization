**APPOINTMENT AND PROMOTION, DISCHARGE and RETIREMENT OF RESERVE OFFICERS.**

Japanese reserve officers are divided into officers of the First Reserve and officers of the Second Reserve. The number of officers in the First Reserve on April 1, 1926 was 22,745 and in the Second Reserve was 15,833.

Reserve officers are junior to officers of the same rank in the active army. They are borne on the list of the division quartered in their native district, but, with the permission of the divisional commander, they may serve in other divisions if it is more convenient for them to do so.

**FIRST RESERVE.**

1. **Appointment of Officers:**

Officers of the First Reserve are drawn from four classes, that is:

(a) One year volunteers, who have passed the necessary examinations.

(b) Officers transferred from the active army.

(c) Warrant officers and noncommissioned officers of the First Reserve who are considered fit for promotion to commissioned rank.

(d) War time appointment.

The present regulations pertaining to each of the four classes enumerated above are as follows:

(a) **One Year Volunteer System:**

"He who, being full seventeen years or more old, but under twenty-one, is a graduate from a government school or an elective course, normal school, middle sdhool or a school of the middle school standard prescribed by Imperial Ordinance, or who is studying at one of the above mentioned schools and can graduate and join the colors before attaining the age of 22 years, can serve with the colors by volunteering for one year's service in the army if he pays the expenses of rations, clothing and equipment during his service andis desirous of becoming a combatant or non-combatant officer of the First or Second Reserve. He is called a one-year volunteer. If he passes an examination at the end of his service with the colors he is appointed a combatant or non-combatant second lieutenant in the First Reserve through the Board of Selection for Officers after going through a four months course of training in the performance of his duties. If he fails in the examination he is made a corporal, combatant or non-combatant." (Part VI, Chapter III, Section 3, Gunsei Gakkyotei, Oct., 1925).

Most of the reserve officers of the junior grades are drawn from one year volunteers. Upon joining a unit these volunteers are graded for four months as ordinary recruits. They are then appointed first class privates and receive instructions from a specially selected officer of the unit. At the end of nine months they may be appointed corporals, but still perform the duties of private soldiers, except for instructional purposes.

(b) **Officers Transferred from the Active Army:**

An officer in the active army is transferred to the First Reserve when he comes under any of the following heads :

(1) When he has passed two years on the unemployed list and is not ordered to take office except when he is made a civilian in the employ of the army in a position where the appointment is to be filled by an officer or when he is missing on the battlefield.

(2) When he has passed a year on the suspended list and is not ordered to take office.

(3) When he is not ordered to take office after passing two years in all on the waiting and unemployment lists, on the unemployed or suspended lists, or on the waiting, [struck out: xxxx] unemployed and suspended lists, except when he is made a civilian in the employ of the army in a position where the position is to be filled by an officer or when he is missing on the battlefield.

(4) When appointed exclusively an official outside of the army, except in the case where the office is one to be filled by an officer.

(5) When he, being a count, viscount, or baron, is elected as a member of the House of Peers.

In addition to the above, an officer may be transferred to the First Reserve at his own request when he is incapicitated for active service by wounds or illness; or he can be made to transfer to the First Reserve by Imperial advice if he is a general officer, or by the advice of the Minister of War if he is a field or company officer, when he is on the waiting list after attaining the numberof years service entitling him to a pension on release, and his state of health or the supply of personnel requires his withdrawal from active service.

Officers are placed on the waiting, unemployed, and suspended lists for the following reasons:

**Waiting List** -

An officer in active service is placed on the waiting list when he comes under any of the following heads and is not ordered to resume office:

(1) When his unit is disbanded or when his government office or post is abolished.

(2) When his course of study is up, or when he comes home from abroad where he prosecuted studies by permission.

(3) When, after he was made a supernumerary by virtue of law, the cause for keeping such a supernumerary has ceased to exist.

(4) When he, after having been given a civil appointment that should be held by an officer, is relieved from that civil appointment.

**Unemployed List** -

An officer in the active service is placed on the unemployed list when he comes under any of the following heads:

(1) When he has passed a year on the waiting list and is not ordered to take office.

(2) When made a civilian employee of the army except in the case where the appointment is one to be held by an officer.

(c) **Warrant Officers and Noncommissioned Officers of the First Reserve Who Are Considered Fit for Promotion to Commissioned Ranks**

The promotion of warrant and noncommissioned officers in time of peace to the gradeof second lieutenant in the First Reserve is made by selection from among those warrant and noncommissioned officers of the First Reserve who are considered especially fit for commission. This appointment is made only after examination for promotion.

In time of war or emergency warrant and noncommissioned officers who hold certificates of fitness for officers may be made probational officers in the First Reserve.

2. **Promotion:**

No officer in the First or Second Reserve is promoted in time of peace except when he is called out for review practice given especially for promotion purposes, or he holds an appointment in a reserve unit. Promotion of reserve officers, if made at all in time of peace, is entirely by selection.

Promotion of officers in the First Reserve in time of war or emergency is made similar to that of officers of the active army. In addition to this normal procedure, an officer in time of war or emergency may be promoted for distinguished service in face of the enemy, for being wounded in service and for bravery.

4. **Training:**

The new Military Service Law provides that men of the reserve may be called out for training not to exceed five times during their total period of service in both the First and Second Reserves. This training is givenfor periods not exceeding thirty-five days in any one year. (Article 55, Milie tary Service Law).

The present practice in regard to training reserve officers, particularly those commissioned as such from one-year volunteers, is to cause the reserve officers to report to units for a three weeks course of training three years after they are commissioned. Thereafter, after the expiration of three years they are again called upon to report for another three weeks course of training.

5. **Discharge and Retirement:**

An officer of theFirst Reserve is transferred to the Second Reserve when he has completed his term of service in the First Reserve.

The end of the term of service for officers of the First Reserve is March 31st of the year following the year in which they attain their respective age limits for active service. In the caseof an officer who was a one-year volunteer the end of the term of service in the First Reserve is March 31st of the seventh (7th) year counted from the year in which his term of service with the colors expired.

An officer of the First Reserve can be placed on the retired list if he is permanently incapacitated for service by reason of wounds or disease.

An officer of the Second Reserve is transferred to the retired list when he has completed his term of service in the Second Reserve. However, he may at his own request, be permitted to serve in the Second Reserve after the expiration of the prescribed term of service.

The end of the term of service for officers in the Second Reserve is March 31st of the sixth (6th) year counted from the year in which they attained their respective age limits for active service. In the case of an officer who was a one-year volunteer, the end of the term ofservice in the Second Reserve is March 31st of the eleventh (11th) year counted from the year in whichhis term of service in the First Reserve expired.
